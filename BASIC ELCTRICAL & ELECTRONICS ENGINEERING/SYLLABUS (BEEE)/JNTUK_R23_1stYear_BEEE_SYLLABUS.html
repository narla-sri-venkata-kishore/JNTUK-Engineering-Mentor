Machines & Measuring Instruments

UNIT II Electrical Machines & Instruments 14 Topics ▾
⚙ ELECTRICAL MACHINES
🔋
DC Motor
Converts electrical energy to mechanical energy. Works on Fleming's Left Hand Rule. Types: Series, Shunt, Compound. Applications: Cranes, traction.
⚡
DC Generator
Converts mechanical to electrical. EMF: E = PΦNZ/60A. Works on Faraday's law. Types: Separately excited, self-excited (series/shunt).
🔄
Transformer
EMF eq: E = 4.44fΦmN. Works on mutual induction. Ratio: V1/V2 = N1/N2. Applications: Power transmission, isolation.
🌀
Induction Motor (3φ)
Self-starting, no brushes. Works on rotating magnetic field. Slip = (Ns-Nr)/Ns. Applications: Pumps, compressors, fans.
🔆
Alternator
Converts mechanical to AC. EMF: E = 4.44fΦmNKw. Synchronous speed Ns = 120f/P. Applications: Power stations.

🛡 SAFETY MEASURES
💥
Fuse
Thin wire that melts when current exceeds safe limit. Breaks circuit permanently. Must be replaced after operation.
🔧
MCB
Miniature Circuit Breaker. Automatically trips on overload/short circuit. Can be reset manually. More reliable than fuse.
🌍
Earthing
Connects metallic parts to earth. Provides safe path for fault current. Prevents electric shock to users.
⚠️
Electric Shock
Occurs when body becomes part of circuit. First aid: Disconnect supply → CPR if unconscious → Do NOT touch victim directly.

Formula Reference

Ohm's Law
V = IR
V: voltage (V), I: current (A), R: resistance (Ω)
Power
P = VI = I²R
P in Watts, V in Volts, I in Amperes
Energy
E = Pt (Wh)
E = Pt/1000 gives kilowatt-hours (units)
Frequency
f = 1/T
f in Hz, T: time period in seconds
RMS Voltage
Vrms = Vm/√2
≈ 0.707 × peak voltage
Average Value
Vavg = 2Vm/π
≈ 0.637 × peak voltage
Impedance Z
Z = √(R²+X²)
X = XL - XC (net reactance)
Power Factor
pf = cos φ = R/Z
Ranges 0 (reactive) to 1 (resistive)
Active Power P
P = VI cos φ
Real power in Watts (W)
Reactive Power Q
Q = VI sin φ
Reactive power in VAR
Apparent Power S
S = VI = √(P²+Q²)
Apparent power in VA
Transformer EMF
E = 4.44fΦmN
f: frequency, Φm: max flux, N: turns
Transformer Ratio
V₁/V₂ = N₁/N₂
Turns ratio = voltage ratio
Synchronous Speed
Ns = 120f/P
f: frequency (Hz), P: poles
Slip (IM)
s = (Ns-Nr)/Ns
Ns: sync speed, Nr: rotor speed
Electricity Units
E = W×H×D/1000
W:watts, H:hours/day, D:days → kWh

How to Score High

📅 Before the Exam

  • Revise all formulas — make a formula sheet
  • Practice derivations (RMS, EMF equation, Superposition)
  • Solve at least 10 numericals per unit
  • Memorize construction diagrams with labels
  • Review comparison tables (PMMC vs MI, Fuse vs MCB)

✍ During the Exam

  • Read all questions first — start with known topics
  • Attempt theory questions with diagrams first
  • Show all steps in numericals — partial marks matter
  • Draw neat, labeled diagrams for every machine
  • Write units with every numerical answer

🎯 Quick Win Topics

  • Electricity bill calculation — always in exam
  • KCL/KVL numericals — straightforward marks
  • EMF equation of transformer — standard derivation
  • Phasor diagrams R, L, C circuits
  • Conventional vs Non-conventional energy comparison
⭐ Scoring Trick: In JNTUK exams, Diagrams + proper headings + step-by-step working + correct units = extra presentation marks even if your answer is partially correct. A neat paper with diagrams always gets 8+ out of 10 for 10-mark questions.
