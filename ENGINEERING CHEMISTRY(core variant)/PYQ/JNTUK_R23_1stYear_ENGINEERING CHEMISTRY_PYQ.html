U1·5M·01Explain the EDTA method for estimation of total hardness of water. Give the stepwise procedure, reactions, and formula used for calculation.
Medium ⭐ Very Frequently Asked
Key Points:
1. Principle: EDTA (Na₂H₂Y) forms stable 1:1 complexes with Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ ions.
2. Indicator: Eriochrome Black T (EBT) — wine red in presence of metal ions, turns blue at endpoint.
3. Buffer: NH₃/NH₄Cl buffer at pH = 10.
4. Reactions: Ca²⁺ + EBT → Ca-EBT (red); Ca-EBT + EDTA → Ca-EDTA + EBT (blue endpoint).
5. Formula: Hardness (ppm) = (Vol. of EDTA × Molarity × M.W. of CaCO₃ × 1000) / Vol. of sample (mL).
Diagram: Burette setup with conical flask containing water sample.
U1·5M·02Numerical: 100 mL of water sample required 15 mL of 0.01 M EDTA for titration using EBT indicator. Calculate the total hardness of water in ppm (as CaCO₃).
Hard ⭐ Very Frequently Asked
Solution:
Hardness = (VEDTA × MEDTA × 100000) / Vsample
= (15 × 0.01 × 100000) / 100
= 150 ppm (as CaCO₃)
U1·5M·03Describe the boiler troubles caused by scale and sludge. Explain their causes, effects, and prevention methods.
Medium ⭐ Very Frequently Asked
Scale: Hard deposits of CaCO₃, CaSO₄, Mg(OH)₂ on inner boiler walls.
Effects of Scale: Overheating → boiler explosion; poor heat transfer → fuel wastage; corrosion under scale.
Prevention: Lime-soda softening, ion exchange, blow-down, anti-scaling chemicals.
Sludge: Soft, loose deposits of MgCO₃, CaCO₃ at cooler regions of boiler.
Prevention: Blow-down (draining), use of coagulants.
U1·5M·04Explain caustic embrittlement of boilers — its mechanism, causes, and methods of prevention. (Diagram required)
Medium Frequently Asked
Mechanism: Concentrated NaOH solution seeps into hair-line cracks in boiler → reacts with iron: Fe + 2NaOH → Na₂FeO₂ + H₂ → embrittlement.
Cause: High concentration of dissolved NaOH at stressed areas of boiler.
Prevention: Add sodium phosphate or tannin to boiler water; use riveted boilers carefully.
U1·5M·05Explain the principle and working of the ion-exchange process for water softening. Write the reactions for cation and anion exchange.
Medium ⭐ Very Frequently Asked
Principle: Ion-exchange resins exchange undesirable ions with H⁺ or OH⁻.
Cation exchange (R-H): 2R-H + Ca²⁺ → R₂Ca + 2H⁺
Anion exchange (R-OH): R-OH + Cl⁻ → R-Cl + OH⁻
H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O (demineralized water)
Regeneration: Cation resin with dil. HCl; Anion resin with dil. NaOH.
U1·5M·06Describe the working of a Reverse Osmosis (RO) plant for desalination with a neat diagram. List its advantages and limitations.
Hard Frequently Asked
Principle: Pressure greater than osmotic pressure forces water molecules through semi-permeable membrane, rejecting dissolved salts.
Membrane: Cellulose acetate or polyamide (TFC).
Diagram: Feed water → High pressure pump → RO membrane → Permeate (pure water) + Brine (reject).
Advantages: Removes 90–99% of dissolved salts, bacteria, viruses. Energy efficient.
Limitations: Membrane fouling, pre-treatment needed, brine disposal problem.
U1·5M·07Explain electrodialysis as a desalination method. Draw the cell diagram and describe ion migration.
Medium Occasionally Asked
Principle: Ionic migration under electric potential through alternating cation and anion exchange membranes.
Working: Salt water in diluting compartments; DC applied → cations move toward cathode through CEM; anions toward anode through AEM → desalted water obtained.
Applications: Brackish water desalination, table salt production.

U1·5M·10Explain the estimation of dissolved oxygen (DO) in water by Winkler's method. Give the reactions involved.
Medium Occasionally Asked
Step 1: MnSO₄ + 2NaOH → Mn(OH)₂ + Na₂SO₄
Step 2: 2Mn(OH)₂ + O₂ → 2MnO(OH)₂ (brown precipitate)

U2·5M·01Derive the Nernst equation for a cell. Apply it to calculate the EMF of the cell: Zn | Zn²⁺(0.01M) || H⁺(1M) | H₂, given E°(Zn²⁺/Zn) = −0.76 V.

U2·5M·08Explain the Pilling-Bedworth ratio. Classify metals as protective and non-protective based on P-B ratio values. Give examples of each type.
HardOccasionally Asked
P-B Ratio = Volume of metal oxide / Volume of metal consumed
P-B < 1: Porous, non-protective film (e.g., Mg, K, Na)
P-B = 1 to 2: Compact, protective film (e.g., Al, Cr, Fe) — passivation
P-B > 2: Flaking, spalling, non-protective (e.g., Mo)
Formula: P-B = (M_oxide × ρ_metal) / (n × M_metal × ρ_oxide)

U2·5M·11Explain the working of a lithium-ion battery with electrode reactions. Why is it preferred over Ni-Cd batteries?

U2·5M·12Explain dry corrosion (high temperature oxidation). Describe the mechanism of oxide film formation on metals with the Pilling-Bedworth criterion.
HardOccasionally Asked
Mechanism: At high temperature, metal reacts directly with O₂ (no electrolyte needed).
M + ½O₂ → MO
Oxide film grows by outward diffusion of metal ions or inward diffusion of O²⁻.
Protective film: Non-porous, adherent, stable → passivation (e.g., Al₂O₃, Cr₂O₃).
Non-protective: Porous, spalling oxide (e.g., FeO, MgO).

U3·5M·04Explain proximate analysis of coal. What information is obtained? Write the step-by-step procedure.
MediumFrequently Asked
Parameters determined: Moisture, Volatile Matter (VM), Ash, Fixed Carbon (FC).
Moisture: Heat coal at 105–110°C for 1hr; % M = (W₁-W₂)/W₁ × 100
VM: Heat residue at 950°C for 7 min without air; % VM = loss - moisture
Ash: Ignite at 700°C completely; % Ash = (ash residue/original weight) × 100
FC = 100 − (M + VM + Ash)
Significance: Determines rank and commercial value of coal.

U3·5M·11What is knocking in IC engines? How are octane and cetane numbers determined? What additives improve them?
HardFrequently Asked

Anti-knock additives: TEL (tetraethyl lead), MMT, MTBE, ethanol blending, iso-octane.

U4·5M·01Explain the setting and hardening of Portland cement with chemical reactions. Why is a controlled water-to-cement ratio important?

U4·5M·04Explain the various mechanisms of lubrication: fluid film, boundary, and extreme pressure lubrication. Describe with appropriate diagrams.
MediumFrequently Asked
Hydrodynamic (thick film): Oil wedge completely separates surfaces; no metal contact; lowest friction.
Boundary lubrication: Oil film too thin; monomolecular layer adsorbed on surface; metal-to-metal contact possible; fatty acids in oil act as boundary lubricants.
Extreme pressure (EP) lubrication: High loads → EP additives (chlorine, S, P compounds) react with metal → form low-shear-strength protective layer.
Diagram: Surface gap comparison for each mechanism.
U4·5M·05Explain the following properties of lubricants with their significance: (i) Viscosity (ii) Flash point (iii) Cloud point (iv) Pour point (v) Saponification value.
Hard⭐ Very Frequently Asked
Viscosity: Resistance to flow; most important property; too high → energy waste; too low → poor film.
Flash Point: Temperature at which oil gives sufficient vapor to flash with flame; safety indicator; good oil has high FP (>200°C).
Cloud Point: Temperature at which wax crystals appear → oil becomes hazy; used for cold-weather applications.
Pour Point: Lowest temperature at which oil flows under gravity; important for cold climates.
Saponification Value: mg KOH needed to saponify 1g oil; high SV → more ester content → better for high-temperature applications.
U4·5M·06Describe the manufacture of Portland cement by dry process. Draw the flow diagram and explain each stage.
MediumFrequently Asked

Stages:
1. Crushing and drying of raw materials
2. Grinding and blending in dry state (raw meal)
3. Preheating in cyclone tower (600°C) → calcination: CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂
4. Rotary kiln (1500°C) → clinker formation: C₃S, C₂S, C₃A, C₄AF
5. Cooling + addition of 2–3% gypsum
6. Grinding to fine powder → Portland cement
Advantage over wet process: Less energy, lower cost.

U5·5M·01Explain the Freundlich adsorption isotherm. Derive its linearized form and show how to verify it graphically. What are its limitations?
Medium⭐ Very Frequently Asked
Equation: x/m = Kp^(1/n) (for gases); x/m = Kc^(1/n) (for solutions)
Log form: log(x/m) = log K + (1/n) log c
Graph: log(x/m) vs log c → straight line; slope = 1/n; intercept = log K
Conditions: n > 1 for favorable; x/m = amount adsorbed per gram adsorbent
Limitations: Empirical (not based on mechanism); fails at high pressures/concentrations; doesn't predict saturation.
U5·5M·02Explain the Langmuir adsorption isotherm. State its assumptions and derive the equation for monolayer adsorption. How does it overcome Freundlich's limitations?
Hard⭐ Very Frequently Asked
Assumptions: Monolayer adsorption; uniform surface; no lateral interaction; dynamic equilibrium.
Rate of adsorption: r_ads = k_a·p(1−θ); Rate of desorption: r_des = k_d·θ
At equilibrium: θ = bp/(1+bp) where b = k_a/k_d
Linear form: p/x = 1/(x_m·b) + p/x_m → Plot p/x vs p → slope = 1/x_m; intercept = 1/(x_m·b)
Advantage: Predicts saturation (θ→1 at high p); has physical basis.
U5·5M·03Describe the preparation of nanomaterials by chemical methods: (a) Chemical reduction (b) Sol-gel process (c) Chemical vapour deposition.
MediumFrequently Asked
(a) Chemical reduction: Metal salt + reducing agent → metal nanoparticles. E.g., HAuCl₄ + sodium citrate → Au nanoparticles (Turkevich method). Citrate acts as capping agent.
(b) Sol-gel: Metal alkoxide precursor + water → hydrolysis → gel → controlled drying → nanoporous oxide. Used for SiO₂, TiO₂ nanoparticles.
(c) CVD: Volatile precursor gases decompose at high temp on substrate → thin film/nanostructure deposition. Used for CNTs, ZnO nanowires.
U5·5M·04Explain Bragg's method for determination of crystal structure by X-ray diffraction. State Bragg's law and explain the experimental setup.
Hard⭐ Very Frequently Asked
Bragg's Law: 2d sinθ = nλ (where d = inter-planar spacing, θ = glancing angle, n = order, λ = X-ray wavelength)
Experimental setup: X-ray source → crystal → ionization detector (or photographic plate)
Crystal rotated → different planes reflect at specific θ → peaks recorded.
Application: Determine d-spacings → crystal structure; identify materials; determine lattice parameters.
Diagram: X-ray beam reflecting off crystal planes with path difference (2d sinθ = nλ shown).
U5·5M·05Explain the preparation and properties of colloidal systems. Describe the methods of stabilization of colloids (electrostatic and steric).
MediumFrequently Asked
Preparation: (a) Dispersion: mechanical grinding, peptization; (b) Condensation: chemical reduction, double decomposition.
Electrostatic stabilization: Double electric layer (Stern layer + diffuse layer) around particle → zeta potential → repulsion between particles.
Steric stabilization: Polymer chains adsorbed on surface create steric barrier against aggregation.
Coagulation/Precipitation: Adding electrolyte collapses double layer → Hardy-Schulze rule: higher valence ion → lower concentration needed.
U5·5M·06Explain the BET equation for surface area determination (no derivation). How is it applied experimentally? State its significance over Langmuir isotherm.
MediumFrequently Asked
BET Equation (Brunauer-Emmett-Teller):
p/[v(p₀−p)] = 1/(v_m·c) + [(c−1)/(v_m·c)] × (p/p₀)
v = volume adsorbed; v_m = monolayer capacity; p₀ = saturation pressure; c = BET constant.
Graph: p/[v(p₀−p)] vs p/p₀ → straight line → slope and intercept give v_m.
Surface area = v_m × N_A × σ (N₂ cross section = 16.2 Å²)
Advantage over Langmuir: Accounts for multilayer adsorption; more accurate for porous materials.
