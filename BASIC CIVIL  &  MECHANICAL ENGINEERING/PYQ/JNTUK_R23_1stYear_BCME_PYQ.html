5-Mark Questions
U1 · Q13 · 5-Mark
Explain the role of Civil Engineers in modern society with at least four real-world contributions.
Medium Very Frequently Asked
Civil Engineers contribute to: (1) Design and construction of infrastructure (roads, bridges, buildings). (2) Water supply and sanitation systems. (3) Environmental protection through waste treatment. (4) Transportation networks enabling economic development. (5) Disaster-resistant structures (earthquake, flood management). They bridge science and society by creating safe, sustainable built environments.
U1 · Q14 · 5-Mark
Describe the properties, composition, and uses of cement concrete. Draw a neat labeled sketch of a concrete mix.
Medium Very Frequently Asked
📐[Diagram: Concrete Mix – Cement + Sand + Aggregate + Water]
Concrete = Cement + Fine Aggregate (sand) + Coarse Aggregate (gravel/crushed stone) + Water. Properties: High compressive strength (~20–40 MPa), durability, fire resistance, workability. Cement paste binds aggregates. Uses: Foundations, slabs, columns, roads, dams. Key parameter: w/c ratio (lower → stronger). Grade M20 = 20 MPa characteristic strength.
U1 · Q15 · 5-Mark
Explain any three disciplines of Civil Engineering with their scope and applications.
Medium Very Frequently Asked
1. Structural Engineering: Design of load-bearing structures (bridges, buildings, dams). Uses mechanics, materials science. 2. Geotechnical Engineering: Studies soil/rock behavior for foundations, embankments, tunnels. Tools: Boring, SPT, plate load test. 3. Transportation Engineering: Design of highways, railways, airports. Considers pavement design, traffic flow, safety. Each discipline ensures infrastructure is safe, economical, and functional.
U1 · Q16 · 5-Mark
Compare the properties of brick, cement, and steel as construction materials. Which would you choose for a high-rise building and why?
Hard Occasionally Asked
Brick: Good compressive strength (~3.5–35 MPa), thermal insulation, eco-friendly, low tensile strength. Cement: Binding agent, gains strength with hydration, used in concrete. Steel: High tensile & compressive strength (~250–500 MPa), ductile, expensive, prone to corrosion. For high-rise: Steel (structural frame) + concrete (core) + brick/glass (cladding) — composite approach gives strength, ductility, and economy.
U1 · Q17 · 5-Mark
Write short notes on prefabricated construction techniques. Mention at least three advantages and two disadvantages.
Medium Frequently Asked
Prefabrication: Structural components (panels, beams, slabs) manufactured in factory, transported and assembled on site. Advantages: (1) Faster construction, (2) Quality control in factory, (3) Reduced labor on site, (4) Less waste. Disadvantages: (1) High initial investment in molds/machinery, (2) Transportation challenges for large elements, (3) Limited design flexibility. Used in: Mass housing, bridges, industrial sheds.
U1 · Q18 · 5-Mark
Explain the importance of Environmental Engineering as a branch of Civil Engineering. How does it address pollution control?
Medium Frequently Asked
Environmental Engineering deals with: (1) Water treatment and distribution, (2) Wastewater treatment (sewage), (3) Air pollution control, (4) Solid waste management, (5) Environmental impact assessment. Pollution control: Designing treatment plants (sedimentation, coagulation, filtration, chlorination for water). Protecting ecosystems through sustainable design. Essential for public health and sustainable urban development.
U1 · Q19 · 5-Mark
A concrete mix has a water-cement ratio of 0.5 and uses 300 kg of cement per m³. Calculate (a) water required per m³ and (b) comment on the expected strength grade.
Hard Frequently Asked
Water = w/c × cement = 0.5 × 300 = 150 kg/m³
(a) Water required = 0.5 × 300 = 150 kg/m³ (or 150 litres/m³). (b) w/c = 0.5 is a moderate ratio. According to IS code, this corresponds approximately to M20–M25 grade concrete (characteristic compressive strength 20–25 MPa at 28 days). Lower w/c → higher strength, but reduced workability. This mix is suitable for RCC structural members.
U1 · Q20 · 5-Mark
Describe the role of Transportation Engineering in economic development of a country with suitable examples.
Medium Frequently Asked
Transportation Engineering designs and manages road, rail, air, and waterway systems. Economic impact: (1) Enables movement of goods and people, reducing trade costs. (2) Connects rural markets to urban centers (e.g., Golden Quadrilateral NH network, India). (3) Creates employment during construction and operation. (4) Attracts industrial investment to connected regions. (5) Airport development boosts tourism and FDI. Example: NH-44 (Srinagar–Kanyakumari) integrating India's economy.
U1 · Q21 · 5-Mark
What are aggregates? Classify them with examples and explain how aggregate size affects concrete mix design.
Medium Occasionally Asked
Aggregates: Granular materials (sand, gravel, crushed stone) occupying ~60–80% of concrete volume. Classification: (1) Fine Aggregate: particle size < 4.75 mm (river sand, M-sand). (2) Coarse Aggregate: particle size > 4.75 mm (20 mm, 40 mm graded). Effect on mix: Larger aggregates → less surface area → less cement paste needed → economical. Smaller aggregates → more workable. Proper grading (IS 383) ensures dense packing, reducing voids and improving strength.
U1 · Q22 · 5-Mark
Explain the scope of Geotechnical Engineering. How do soil properties affect foundation design? Give a real-world application scenario.
Hard Occasionally Asked
Geotechnical Engineering studies soil/rock behavior under loads. Scope: Foundation design, slope stability, retaining walls, embankments, tunnels. Soil properties affecting foundation: (1) Bearing capacity (allowable load per unit area), (2) Settlement characteristics, (3) Shear strength, (4) Permeability, (5) Compressibility. Real-world: Leaning Tower of Pisa — poor geotechnical assessment of soft marine clay led to differential settlement. Modern deep foundation (pile foundations) in Mumbai on soft reclaimed land.
U1 · Q23 · 5-Mark
Describe the physical and chemical properties of bricks. List the field tests performed to assess brick quality.
Medium Frequently Asked
Physical properties: Shape (rectangular), size (190×90×90mm), color (red/cherry), texture (smooth/rough), density (~1800 kg/m³), water absorption (<20%). Chemical: Mainly alumina + silica + lime + oxides of iron. Tests: (1) Water absorption test – immerse 24 hrs, gain < 20% by weight, (2) Compressive strength test (crushing strength > 3.5 MPa for 3rd class), (3) Hardness test – nail scratch, (4) Soundness test – ring when struck, (5) Efflorescence test – white deposits after drying.

U2 · Q13 · 5-Mark
Explain the objectives and principles of surveying. What is the fundamental rule that governs surveying operations?
Medium Very Frequently Asked
Objectives: (1) Determine relative positions of points on earth's surface, (2) Prepare accurate maps/plans, (3) Establish boundaries, (4) Layout engineering works. Principles: (1) Work from Whole to Part — establish control points first, then fill details (prevents accumulation of errors). (2) Check every measurement independently. Instruments: Chain, tape (linear), compass, theodolite (angular), level (vertical). Accuracy is paramount; small errors in control points propagate throughout the survey.
U2 · Q14 · 5-Mark
The following staff readings were recorded from two instrument stations. Compute the RLs using the Height of Instrument method and apply arithmetic check.
BS: 1.850, IS: 1.320, 2.150, FS: 0.985 (TBM = 100.000 m)
Hard Very Frequently Asked
HI = RL of BM + BS | RL = HI – IS/FS | Check: ΣBS – ΣFS = First RL – Last RL
HI = 100.000 + 1.850 = 101.850 m. RL (IS1) = 101.850 – 1.320 = 100.530 m. RL (IS2) = 101.850 – 2.150 = 99.700 m. RL (FS) = 101.850 – 0.985 = 100.865 m. Arithmetic check: ΣBS – ΣFS = 1.850 – 0.985 = 0.865. Last RL – First RL = 100.865 – 100.000 = 0.865 ✓. Check satisfied.
U2 · Q15 · 5-Mark
Explain the characteristics (properties) of contour lines with neat sketches. How are valleys and ridges distinguished in a contour map?
Medium Very Frequently Asked
🗺[Diagram: Contour lines – Hills, Valleys, Ridges, Cliffs]
Properties: (1) Each contour is a closed curve. (2) Two contours cannot cross (except overhanging cliff). (3) Closely spaced = steep slope; widely spaced = gentle slope. (4) Contours do not merge except at vertical cliff. (5) Contour crossing a ridge is U-shaped (pointing downhill); crossing a valley is V-shaped (pointing uphill). Ridge: contours form U opening in direction of higher elevation. Valley: V-shaped contours point towards higher elevation.
U2 · Q16 · 5-Mark
Describe the instruments used in levelling with neat labeled sketches. Explain the procedure of simple levelling.
Medium Frequently Asked
📏[Diagram: Dumpy Level – Telescope, Bubble tube, Levelling screws, Tripod]
Instruments: (1) Dumpy Level – telescope + spirit level + levelling head (most common). (2) Auto Level – self-compensating, faster. (3) Digital Level – electronic staff reading. (4) Levelling Staff (4m telescopic) – graduated in mm. Procedure: Set up level, center bubble, take BS on known BM, read IS on intermediate points, take FS on last point. Calculate HI = RL of BM + BS. Each RL = HI – staff reading. Apply arithmetic check.
U2 · Q17 · 5-Mark
Convert the following WCBs to Reduced Bearings and determine the quadrant for each: (a) 45° (b) 145° (c) 250° (d) 310°
Hard Frequently Asked
NE: RB=WCB | SE: RB=180°–WCB | SW: RB=WCB–180° | NW: RB=360°–WCB
(a) WCB=45°: NE quadrant → RB = N 45° E. (b) WCB=145°: SE quadrant → RB = 180°–145° = S 35° E. (c) WCB=250°: SW quadrant → RB = 250°–180° = S 70° W. (d) WCB=310°: NW quadrant → RB = 360°–310° = N 50° W.
U2 · Q18 · 5-Mark
Explain the methods of locating contours in the field. Describe the indirect method with grid points.
Medium Occasionally Asked
Methods: (1) Direct Method: Instrument set up at known RL; staff moved until required reading obtained — slow, accurate. (2) Indirect Method (Grid): Area divided into squares (5m×5m); RL of each grid point determined by levelling; contours interpolated between points. (3) Radial Method: Rays from central point; RL along each ray surveyed. Grid method is most common for large areas — systematic, fast, can be automated with total station. Contours interpolated by proportional division between grid points.
U2 · Q19 · 5-Mark
What is a compass survey? Describe the prismatic compass and explain how horizontal bearings are measured using it.
Medium Frequently Asked
🧭[Diagram: Prismatic Compass – Prism, Needle, Graduated ring, Object vane]
Compass survey: Measures direction of survey lines relative to magnetic north. Prismatic Compass: (1) Circular graduated ring (0°–360°) attached to magnetic needle, (2) Prism for reading, (3) Object vane with hair sight, (4) Eye vane. Procedure: Set up at station, sight through object vane to target, read bearing through prism. WCB read directly. Accuracy ±30'. Used for: Rough surveys, reconnaissance, forest surveys. Local attraction (iron/steel) must be checked.
U2 · Q20 · 5-Mark
Describe the Rise and Fall method of levelling. Solve: BS=1.5m, IS=0.8m, 1.2m, FS=1.9m. Starting RL=200m. Apply arithmetic check.
Hard Very Frequently Asked
Rise/Fall = prev. reading – next reading | RL(next) = RL(prev) ± Rise/Fall
BS(A)=1.5, IS(B)=0.8: 1.5–0.8=0.7 Rise. IS(B)=0.8, IS(C)=1.2: 0.8–1.2=–0.4 Fall. IS(C)=1.2, FS(D)=1.9: 1.2–1.9=–0.7 Fall. RLs: A=200.0, B=200.7, C=200.3, D=199.6. Arithmetic check: ΣBS–ΣFS=1.5–1.9=–0.4. ΣRise–ΣFall=0.7–(0.4+0.7)=–0.4. Last RL–First RL=199.6–200.0=–0.4 ✓.
U2 · Q21 · 5-Mark
Explain the uses of contour maps in Civil Engineering projects with at least four practical applications.
Medium Occasionally Asked
Uses: (1) Route alignment – select best gradient for roads/railways by following contours. (2) Reservoir capacity – estimate volume of water a dam can store (planimeter area between contours). (3) Cut & fill calculation – earthwork quantities for road construction. (4) Watershed delineation – identify drainage basins for irrigation projects. (5) Slope analysis – identify safe zones for construction vs landslide-prone areas. (6) Intervisibility – determine line-of-sight for communication towers. Contour maps are fundamental to all site-planning decisions.
U2 · Q22 · 5-Mark
Describe the sources of errors in chain surveying. How can these errors be minimized in field work?
Medium Occasionally Asked
Errors: (1) Personal errors – misreading tape, incorrect chaining. (2) Instrument errors – incorrect chain length (standardize regularly). (3) Natural errors – temperature changes, slope not corrected, wind. (4) Booking errors – recording wrong numbers. Minimization: (1) Apply correction for temperature and slope, (2) Keep chain free of kinks, (3) Use ranging rods for straight lines, (4) Check measurement independently (forward and back), (5) Use plumb bob on slopes, (6) Maintain proper field book entries.
U2 · Q23 · 5-Mark
Write short notes on: (a) Total Station (b) GPS in Surveying. How have these modern tools transformed traditional surveying?
Medium Frequently Asked
(a) Total Station: Combines electronic theodolite + EDM + data recorder. Measures angles and distances simultaneously; computes coordinates automatically. Accuracy: 1" angular, 1–2 mm distance. Used for: control surveys, setting out, as-built surveys. (b) GPS: Global Positioning System – 24 satellites. Provides 3D coordinates (X,Y,Z) anywhere on earth. RTK-GPS accuracy: ±1–2 cm. Used for: cadastral surveys, highway alignment, monitoring structural deformation. Transformation: Reduced survey time by 80%, eliminated human reading errors, enabled GIS integration, automated large-scale mapping.

5-Mark Questions
U3 · Q13 · 5-Mark
Explain the structure of Flexible and Rigid pavements with neat labeled cross-section sketches. Compare their suitability for different traffic conditions.
Medium Very Frequently Asked
🛣[Cross-section: Flexible – Subgrade / Sub-base / Base / Bituminous surface] [Rigid – Subgrade / Sub-base / PCC slab]
Flexible: Bituminous (asphalt) surface. Distributes load through layers. Layers: Subgrade → Sub-base (granular) → Base course → Binder course → Surface course. Suitable for: Medium-heavy traffic, flexible under thermal changes. Rigid: PCC (Portland Cement Concrete) slab. Load distributed by slab flexure. Layers: Subgrade → Sub-base → CC slab (200–300 mm). Suitable for: Very heavy loads (trucks, aircraft runways, toll roads). Comparison: Flexible cheaper initially, needs maintenance; Rigid expensive, less maintenance, longer life (30 yr vs 15 yr).
U3 · Q14 · 5-Mark
What is Rainwater Harvesting? Describe any two methods with diagrams. State the benefits of rooftop rainwater harvesting.
Medium Frequently Asked
💧[Diagram: Rooftop RWH – Roof → Gutter → Downpipe → Filter → Storage tank]
RWH: Collecting and storing rainwater from rooftops, land surface, or rock catchments for use. Methods: (1) Rooftop RWH: Roof (catchment) → gutters → downpipes → first-flush device → filter → sump/tank. (2) Surface RWH: Check dams, percolation ponds, farm ponds — capture runoff in low-lying areas. Benefits: (1) Reduces dependence on municipal supply, (2) Recharges groundwater aquifers, (3) Reduces urban flooding, (4) Low cost, sustainable, (5) Reduces soil erosion. Mandatory in many Indian cities for buildings >100 m².
U3 · Q15 · 5-Mark
Explain the basic concepts of Dams and Reservoirs. Classify dams based on material and hydraulic design. Mention uses of a reservoir.
Medium Frequently Asked
Dam: A barrier constructed across a watercourse to store water, regulate flow. Types by material: (1) Earthen dam – most common, cheap, uses local soil. (2) Masonry dam – stone/brick, durable, steep faces. (3) Concrete dam – gravity/arch/buttress. Types by hydraulic action: (1) Gravity dam – resists water by own weight (e.g., Nagarjuna Sagar). (2) Arch dam – transfers load to canyon walls (thin section). (3) Buttress dam – supported by buttresses (hollow, economical). Reservoir uses: Water supply, irrigation, hydropower, flood control, recreation.
U3 · Q16 · 5-Mark
A city has annual rainfall of 750 mm. The rooftop area of a building is 200 m² with a runoff coefficient of 0.85. Estimate (a) annual rooftop runoff volume and (b) daily average available for storage.
Hard Frequently Asked
Runoff = Rainfall × Area × Runoff coefficient
(a) Annual Runoff = 0.75 m × 200 m² × 0.85 = 127.5 m³ = 127,500 litres. (b) Daily average = 127,500 / 365 = ~349 litres/day. This is significant — can meet 3–4 people's daily needs (100 L/day/person). Note: First flush (3–5 mm) should be diverted away to remove contaminants. Actual collection will be seasonal; design storage for dry months.
U3 · Q17 · 5-Mark
Explain the introduction to Airport Engineering. What are the major components of an airport? Describe the factors considered while selecting an airport site.
Medium Occasionally Asked
✈[Layout: Runway, Taxiway, Apron, Terminal, Control Tower, Hangar]
Airport Engineering: Planning, design, construction of airport facilities. Components: (1) Runway – main landing/takeoff strip (length: 1500–4500m), (2) Taxiway – connects runway to apron, (3) Apron – aircraft parking and boarding area, (4) Terminal Building – passenger processing, (5) Control Tower – air traffic control, (6) Hangar – maintenance. Site selection factors: (1) Level terrain, adequate area, (2) Away from populated areas (noise), (3) Wind direction analysis, (4) Obstruction clearance (no tall buildings), (5) Soil bearing capacity, (6) Accessibility to city (15–30 km), (7) Fog and meteorological conditions.
U3 · Q18 · 5-Mark
Describe the water cycle (hydrological cycle) and explain the concept of hydrology with its importance in water resources management.
Medium Frequently Asked
🌊[Diagram: Hydrological Cycle – Evaporation → Condensation → Precipitation → Runoff → Infiltration]
Hydrology: Science of water – its occurrence, circulation, distribution, properties. Water Cycle: Evaporation (sun heats surface water) → Transpiration (plants) → Evapotranspiration → Condensation (forms clouds) → Precipitation (rain, snow) → Runoff (surface) / Infiltration (groundwater recharge) → River flow → Ocean. Importance: (1) Estimate flood discharge for dam design, (2) Plan irrigation systems, (3) Design urban drainage, (4) Groundwater management, (5) Drought prediction. Key parameters: Rainfall intensity, runoff coefficient, time of concentration.
U3 · Q19 · 5-Mark
Explain the basic principles of Harbour Engineering. What are breakwaters? Describe their types and functions.
Hard Rare
Harbour: A sheltered water body where vessels can anchor safely. Harbour Engineering: Design of docks, breakwaters, quays, navigation channels, berthing facilities. Breakwaters: Structures built to protect harbour from wave action. Types: (1) Rubble-mound breakwater – loose stones/concrete blocks, absorb wave energy, most common. (2) Vertical breakwater – solid concrete wall, reflects waves. (3) Composite – rubble base + concrete cap. Functions: (1) Reduce wave height inside harbour, (2) Create calm water for loading/unloading, (3) Protect ships from storm surges, (4) Create navigable channel. Example: Visakhapatnam Port breakwater – 3 km long rubble-mound structure.
U3 · Q20 · 5-Mark
Write notes on Tunnel Engineering. What are the methods of tunnelling? Mention two advantages of tunnels over surface roads in hilly terrain.
Medium Occasionally Asked
Tunnel: Underground passage through a hill, mountain, river bed, or urban area. Methods: (1) Cut and Cover – excavate trench, construct structure, backfill (used in cities, shallow depth). (2) Drill and Blast – drill holes, place explosives, blast, remove muck (hard rock). (3) TBM (Tunnel Boring Machine) – mechanized excavation, circular cross-section, fast, minimal disturbance. (4) NATM (New Austrian Tunnelling Method) – uses rock strength, shotcrete lining. Advantages in hilly terrain: (1) Shorter route (avoids mountain detour), (2) No switchback curves (safer), (3) All-weather connectivity, (4) Reduces ecological disturbance vs open cut. Example: Banihal Tunnel (NH-44), J&K.
U3 · Q21 · 5-Mark
Explain Railway Engineering basics. What are the components of a railway track? Distinguish between broad gauge and metre gauge.
Medium Occasionally Asked
Railway Track Components: (1) Rails – steel sections (52 kg/m, 60 kg/m), carry wheel load. (2) Sleepers (ties) – timber/concrete/steel, hold rails at correct gauge, distribute load to ballast. (3) Ballast – crushed stone (40mm size), distributes load, provides drainage, holds sleepers. (4) Fish plates – connect rail ends. (5) Spikes/rail clips – fix rails to sleepers. (6) Formation – compacted earthwork below ballast. Gauge: Broad Gauge (1676 mm, Indian standard) – higher speed, capacity. Metre Gauge (1000 mm) – narrower, economical for lower traffic. Indian Railways converting all MG lines to BG (Project Unigauge).
U3 · Q22 · 5-Mark
What are the BIS specifications for drinking water quality? Describe the permissible limits of pH, turbidity, hardness, and any two chemical parameters.
Hard Frequently Asked
IS 10500:2012 – Indian Standard for Drinking Water Specifications
IS 10500:2012 Permissible Limits: (1) pH: 6.5–8.5 (desirable: 7.0–8.5). (2) Turbidity: ≤1 NTU (desirable), max 5 NTU. (3) Total Hardness: ≤200 mg/L (desirable), max 600 mg/L. (4) Total Dissolved Solids: ≤500 mg/L (desirable), max 2000 mg/L. (5) Chlorides: ≤250 mg/L (desirable), max 1000 mg/L. (6) Fluoride: 1 mg/L (desirable), max 1.5 mg/L (excess causes fluorosis). (7) Coliform bacteria: Absent in 100 mL sample. Physical tests: Color, Odor, Taste. Chemical: BOD, DO, heavy metals (Pb, As, Hg – trace limits).
U3 · Q23 · 5-Mark
Explain the importance of Transportation Engineering in the economic development of India. Give four specific examples of infrastructure projects.
Medium Frequently Asked

Compare the Diesel cycle and Otto cycle. A Diesel engine has r=16, cut-off ratio r_c=2.5 (γ=1.4). Find its thermal efficiency.
Hard Very Frequently Asked
η_Diesel = 1 – [1/r^(γ-1)] × [(r_c^γ – 1)/(γ(r_c – 1))]
η = 1 – [1/16^0.4] × [(2.5^1.4 – 1)/(1.4×(2.5–1))]. 16^0.4 = 3.031. 2.5^1.4 = 3.251. η = 1 – (1/3.031) × [(3.251–1)/(1.4×1.5)] = 1 – 0.330 × [2.251/2.1] = 1 – 0.330 × 1.072 = 1 – 0.354 = 0.646 = 64.6%. U6 · Q16 · 5-Mark
Explain the working of a Hydro Electric Power Plant. Describe its major components and the principle of energy conversion.
Medium Very Frequently Asked
💧[Layout: Reservoir → Dam → Penstock → Turbine → Generator → Transformer → Grid]
Hydro Power: Converts potential energy of water (stored at height) → kinetic → mechanical → electrical. Components: (1) Reservoir – stores water (head = height), (2) Dam – creates water head, controls flow, (3) Intake/Gate – controls water flow, (4) Penstock – large pipe delivering water to turbine at high velocity, (5) Turbine – water drives runner (Pelton for high head, Francis/Kaplan for medium/low), (6) Draft tube – gradually slows water after turbine (energy recovery), (7) Generator – turbine shaft drives alternator, (8) Transformer – steps up voltage for transmission. Energy conversion: PE = mgh → KE → Mechanical (shaft rotation) → Electrical. Efficiency: 85–95% (highest of all power plants). Zero fuel cost, no emissions. Examples: Bhakra Dam (1325 MW), Tehri Dam (2400 MW).
U6 · Q17 · 5-Mark
Explain Gear Drives — types of gears, gear terminology (module, pitch circle, pressure angle), and a gear train with numerical example.
Medium Frequently Asked
⚙[Diagram: Spur gear pair – Pitch circles, addendum, dedendum, module, pressure angle]
Types: Spur (parallel shafts, straight teeth), Helical (parallel shafts, inclined teeth, quieter), Bevel (intersecting shafts, conical), Worm (high ratio, self-locking), Rack & pinion (rotary → linear). Terminology: Module m = D/T (D=pitch circle dia, T=number of teeth). Pitch circle: reference circle where gears mesh. Pressure angle (20° standard): angle between line of action and common tangent. Addendum: tooth height above pitch circle. Dedendum: tooth depth below pitch circle. Clearance: addendum – dedendum. Gear train: If T₁=20 teeth, T₂=60, N₁=1500 RPM: Velocity ratio = T₁/T₂ = 20/60 = 1/3. N₂ = 1500/3 = 500 RPM. For compound gear train, multiply individual ratios. Applications: Automotive gearbox, clock mechanisms, winches, machine tools.

U6 · Q19 · 5-Mark
Explain the working of a Nuclear Power Plant. What is nuclear fission? Describe the role of the reactor, moderator, control rods, and coolant.
Hard Frequently Asked
⚛[Diagram: Nuclear reactor core → Heat exchanger → Steam turbine → Generator]
Nuclear Fission: Splitting of heavy nucleus (U-235, Pu-239) by slow neutron → two lighter nuclei + 3 neutrons + enormous energy (E=mc²). Chain reaction: Released neutrons cause further fissions. Controlled chain reaction → heat → steam → turbine → electricity. Reactor components: (1) Fuel rods: U-235 enriched uranium pellets in zirconium alloy tubes. (2) Moderator: Slows neutrons to thermal speeds – heavy water (CANDU) or graphite (older), light water (PWR). (3) Control rods: Boron/cadmium rods absorb neutrons – inserted/withdrawn to regulate reaction rate. (4) Coolant: Removes heat from core – light water (PWR, BWR), heavy water (CANDU). (5) Pressure vessel: Containment at high pressure. (6) Steam generator: Transfers heat to secondary water loop → steam → turbine. India: Kudankulam NPP (VVER-1000), Tarapur. Capacity: 1000+ MW per unit. Zero CO₂ emissions; concern: radioactive waste disposal.
U6 · Q20 · 5-Mark
Explain Chain Drive — types of chains, advantages over belt drives, and applications. Where is chain drive preferred over gear drive?
Medium Occasionally Asked
Chain Drive: Positive drive – chain links mesh with sprocket teeth. No slip → exact velocity ratio. Types: (1) Roller chain (most common): Bush roller over each pin – good for 1–100 kW, up to 50 m/s. (2) Silent/Inverted tooth chain: Tooth-shaped links – quieter at high speeds. (3) Leaf chain: Used in forklifts for lifting. Advantages over belt: (1) No slip – exact ratio, (2) Transmits more power in same cross-section, (3) Operates at higher temperatures, (4) Suitable for wet/oily conditions, (5) No creep. Chain vs Gear: Chain preferred for: (1) Long centre distances (belts slip, gears impractical), (2) Moderate power (gears for very high power at short distance). Examples: Bicycle, motorcycle final drive, conveyor systems, agricultural machinery, forklifts.
U6 · Q21 · 5-Mark
A gear train has 3 gears: T₁=25, T₂=75, T₃=50 teeth. If N₁=1200 RPM, find N₃. If the gear 2 is an idler gear, what is the direction of rotation of gear 3? Also find the overall velocity ratio.
Hard Frequently Asked
Overall VR = N₁/N₃ = (T₂/T₁) × (T₃/T₂) = T₃/T₁ (idler has no effect on VR)
Idler gear (G2) is between G1 and G3, meshing with both. N₂ = N₁ × T₁/T₂ = 1200 × 25/75 = 400 RPM. N₃ = N₂ × T₂/T₃ = 400 × 75/50 = 600 RPM. Alternatively, N₃ = N₁ × T₁/T₃ = 1200 × 25/50 = 600 RPM. VR (overall) = N₁/N₃ = 1200/600 = 2:1. Direction: G1 clockwise → G2 anticlockwise → G3 clockwise (same as G1). Idler reverses direction twice → net: same direction as driver. Idler changes direction but NOT overall velocity ratio. Application: Lathe back-gear, reversing mechanisms in machine tools.
